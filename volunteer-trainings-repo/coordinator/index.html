01

Role Overview

Coordinators are the connective layer between the Medical Group Leads and the wider event team. You do not replace a Group Lead's authority over their own team — you make sure every team is supported, on schedule, and able to escalate fast when it matters.

  • Shared function — support the Medical Group Lead and ensure medics take their assigned breaks via their Group Lead.
  • Two Coordinator roles — Crowd Medic Coordinator and Roaming Medic Coordinator, each owns a distinct subunit.
  • Reporting line — Coordinators → Group Leads → Volunteers. All Coordinators report directly to the HOD / Team Leads.

You are the escalation point

When a Group Lead needs support, direction, or an outside resource, they come to you.

03

Crowd Medic Coordinator

Lunch break rota & escalations.

  • Break rota — one section leaves post at a time for 15 minutes. Nearby sections cover until the team returns. Eateries are positioned in the concourses.
  • Escalations — pass key information to stewards immediately, so external medical help can be reached as soon as possible.
  • Testimony escort — once your attention is given, escort the testifier from the Group Lead to the nearest allocated pastors' location.
  • Incident documentation — ensure your Group Leads are documenting all incidents via the incident form; don't let this slip to end of day.
  • Section awareness — watch out for information coming from the team leads in the sections you are assigned to.
04

Roaming Medic Coordinator

Positioning, forms & break cover.

  • Positioning — confirm all four Roaming teams are in position before the programme begins, and that they have the support they need.
  • Incident forms — ensure Group Leads are documenting all incidents via the incident form, completed by every team before the end of the day.
  • Breaks — each Roaming team breaks once every hour for 20 minutes before returning to patrol.
  • Kit cover — while a team is on break, you physically hold their kit and hand it back on their return.
  • Accessible exits & guest experience — Roaming teams also cover the accessible exits and entries (AE1, AE2) in support of the guest experience.

08

Coordinator Code of Conduct

1
Safeguard First

Testifiers and vulnerable guests are never left unaccompanied. Report any safeguarding concern immediately.

2
Represent the Team

Stay calm, professional, and Christ-centred at all times — you represent the Medical Team to guests, stewards, and leadership.

3
Escalate, Don't Improvise

If something is outside your remit, escalate through the chain rather than acting alone.

4
Documentation Integrity

Ensure every incident form your Group Leads handle is completed accurately, and follow up if one is missing.

5
Confidentiality

Testimony and incident details are shared only with those who need them — Pastors, Group Leads, event leadership.

6
Be Present, Be Reachable

Stay visible and reachable to your Group Leads throughout your shift; never leave your zone uncovered.

09

Code of Conduct — Leading With Character

1
Serve With Humility

Your position is a stewardship, not a status. Be first to arrive, last to leave, and willing to do what you ask of others.

2
Integrity and Honesty

Report accurately to Leadership, even when the news is difficult. Own your mistakes, and guard confidential matters carefully.

3
Delegate Clearly

Give Leads clear scope, expectations, and deadlines. Put key decisions and instructions in writing so nothing is lost to memory.

4
Support Without Micromanaging

Check in consistently, but trust Leads to run their own teams. Correct privately, encourage publicly.

5
Report With Clarity

Give Leadership timely updates and bring solutions, not only problems. Distinguish facts, assumptions, and recommendations.

6
Unity and Servant Leadership

Champion your Leads to Leadership, and Leadership's vision to your Leads. Lead by doing, not demanding.

12

Documenting a Medical Incident

Every guest interaction is someone's whole day. A clear, written record protects them, protects you, and keeps the whole medics chain working as one team.

  • Continuity of care — anyone who picks up the case afterward (Group Lead, Coordinator, stadium medical team) knows exactly what happened and what's already been done.
  • Accountability — a written, timestamped record shows the right steps were followed and the right people were informed.
  • Safeguarding — protects the guest, especially where next-of-kin, hospital transfer, or a vulnerable person is involved.
  • Learning for next time — patterns across incidents help the HOD improve training, staffing, and stadium liaison for future conferences.

Golden rule

One form per incident — even minor ones. If it needed a medical assessment, it needs a form. A quick check that turns out fine still gets logged; it protects you and the guest either way.

14

Incident & Person Details

The who, where, and when — get this down first, exactly as it happened.

1. Incident Details +
  • Date and time — as close to the actual moment as possible.
  • Stand / Block, and row or seat if known — precise location speeds up any stadium escalation.
  • Reported by — your name, as the attending Medic.
  • Group Lead and Coordinator notified — name who you told, not just "yes".
2. Person Details +
  • Name and approx. age, if you can get them without pressuring a distressed guest.
  • Guest / Volunteer / Staff — changes who else needs informing.
  • Known medical conditions — ask gently; this can change your assessment.
  • Next of kin contacted? — mark clearly if this is still outstanding.
15

Presenting Signs

Two checklists, kept separate on purpose.

  • Unresponsive / no chest movement
  • Collapse / loss of consciousness
  • Seizure-like symptoms
  • Difficulty breathing or gasping
  • Chest pain or clutching chest
  • Severe confusion or unresponsiveness
  • Bluish lips or very pale skin

Spiritual manifestation (for context, not diagnosis)

Shaking · Collapse · Agitation · Unresponsiveness
Note context only — this never replaces the medical assessment. Medical checks always come first.

17

Outcome, Sign-off & Before You Go Out

Close the loop — this is what makes the record usable afterward.

  • Resolved on site
  • Returned to seat
  • Transferred to stadium medical
  • Taken to hospital

Sign-off

No names logged, no closed incident. Medic, Group Lead, and Coordinator names are what make the record official.

Before You Go Out on Shift

  • Medical assessment always comes first.
  • One form, every incident, filled in on site.
  • Name who you escalated to — every time.
  • Submit the form on your phone the moment it's handled — don't batch incidents.
  • Save your form link or QR code before your shift starts.
  • Ask your Coordinator if you're ever unsure.
